## Round 2 — Deep-response form (1 response · R2)
- **Widely-accepted idea that's wrong:** "AI is close to replacing human thinking in paid advertising." AI is valuable and used every day to speed up analysis, generate ideas, improve efficiency — but it shouldn't lead decision-making. The best results come from people who understand the client's business, ask the right questions, challenge assumptions, and interpret data in context. AI identifies patterns but doesn't understand business objectives. AI should amplify human expertise, not replace it.
- **Best practice that needs a warning label:** Platform recommendations. Always review them, rarely implement without checking alignment to business goals. Platforms recommend budget increases even at ROAS target/limit; Meta suggests AI creative changes that don't fit the brand; TikTok has recommended pausing campaigns performing exactly as intended. The label: "Recommendations are based on algorithms, not on your client's business context." They should inform decisions, not make them.
- **Most-asked client questions:** (Operational role, no direct client contact — routes to AMs.)
- **Programmatic wallpaper take:** "Programmatic is all about automation and AI making the best decisions." Reframe: programmatic isn't about replacing marketers — it's about giving marketers better tools to make smarter decisions at scale. Strategy, context, and critical thinking separate good campaigns from great.
- **Team to feature:** [team member].
- **News sources:** Google Ads Blog & Search Central; Meta / TikTok / LinkedIn business updates; LinkedIn professionals sharing real case studies; hands-on testing across Google, Meta, TikTok, DV360.
- **Voices watched:** Practitioners in Canada (and international networks).
- **Uncomfortable truth:** Paid media can't fix a bad product, a weak offer, or a poor customer experience. It doesn't create value — it amplifies what's already there. Great marketing scales success; it doesn't create it.
- **"We want to be on every channel":** The goal isn't to be everywhere — it's to be where your audience is and where you generate the greatest business impact. Depends on brand maturity (awareness before conversion for new brands). Channel selection is strategic: audience, objectives, budget, journey role.
- **$50K/month — stop first:** Making decisions on assumptions instead of data. Understand business goals, attribution, customer journey, historical performance, channel contribution to revenue. Then find waste (misaligned campaigns, overlapping audiences, wrong channels, non-incremental spend) and reallocate to the highest return — not just cut.

## Round 3 — Deep-response form (3 responses)

### R3-A
- **Widely-accepted idea that's wrong:** "AI is close to replacing creative teams." AI is good at variation, not differentiation — great at speeding execution of a strong concept, not creating the concept. The human touch isn't going away.
- **Best practice / warning label:** Automated bidding tools need oversight. Seen constantly with lead quality: volume supersedes quality; the algorithm optimizes toward CPL and delivers cheaper leads. Looks good on paper, then downstream (sales, CS, finance) finds the quality gap. Unchecked automation is riskier than a normal mistake because it doesn't surface right away.
- **Most-asked client questions:** How to get ahead of AI Overviews before they hurt search, and how to show up in them. Alphabet's earnings confirmed AI Overviews are pulling search share; Google says same monetization rate but hasn't solved the business model. Clients rightly have no clear POV yet.
- **Programmatic wallpaper:** "Data-driven targeting." Used for everything from first-party purchase data to a modeled lookalike — very different things. Say the specific thing: "targeting based on last week's purchase data" or "a modelled lookalike audience."
- **Wins / learnings:** KPI reframing with SkiBig3. Friction from pushing higher ROAS every year, which works against revenue growth. Asked the client why they wanted more efficiency; answer: ROAS is easiest for stakeholders (ownership, funders) to understand and justify budget. Reframed from "us vs. them" to "help me help you." Landed on modeling lodging revenue at different budget tiers (a number all stakeholders agree on), plus LTV and new-customer acquisition, trended over time. Now: at a higher budget tier, expect X new customers, LTV grows X%, compounding to X% more revenue over two years. ROAS becomes the floor (minimum for profitability), benchmarks set on growth segments.
- **Team to feature:** [team members] (outside leadership).
- **News sources:** AdExchanger; Marketing Brew; Search Engine Land; Reddit PPC.
- **Voices / competitors:** Newsletters & competitors (Major Tom, Thrive). Likes Thrive's LinkedIn — team members doing video walkthroughs to build connection/trust/thought leadership.
- **Uncomfortable truth:** Attribution models are flawed, and most brands aren't running incrementality testing. Last click gets the credit; it doesn't show what's incremental or what would have occurred organically.
- **"We want to be on every channel":** Not every channel serves the same purpose. Awareness, consideration, and conversion don't live on the same platforms. Budget dilution drops reach everywhere. The question isn't which channels you can be on — it's which you can fund well enough to move the number. Fewer channels done properly beats more channels without sufficient budget.
- **Most-asked (visitation):** How to translate advertising into actual visitation (tourism concentration). Ad platforms report on-platform actions, not foot traffic / hotel occupancy / visitor counts, unless someone builds the bridge (geo-conversion data, visitation studies, matching campaign timing to destination-level visitor data). Still researching internally.
- **Event:** BCAMA — "AI: The Biggest Shift."
- **$50K — stop first:** Not enough context without an audit. Start by asking questions, then an honest assessment (campaign structure, whether objectives drive the business, whether setup was built for success), then recommend.

### R3-B
- **Widely-accepted idea that's wrong:** The predictable funnel (awareness → consideration → conversion). Modern journeys are non-linear and multi-touch (TikTok discovery → Google search → reviews → ignore retargeting → email return → compare competitors → YouTube → branded search). The customer journey is a dynamic, multi-touch path, not a funnel. Understand the role each channel plays; give algorithms freedom inside a strategy. Advertisers are no longer media managers/buyers/planners — they are strategic partners designing a system that guides both the customer journey and the algorithms toward a business outcome.
- **Best practice / warning label:** Dynamic creative should be grounded in a clear creative strategy. Minor cosmetic changes (colours, layouts) don't give platforms enough differentiation. Go beyond superficial variation — distinct messaging, value props, benefits, concepts for different segments and journey stages. Don't use dynamic creative to inflate asset counts; use it to scale a deliberate strategy.
- **Most-asked client question:** "How can we get the same results with less investment?"
- **Programmatic wallpaper:** "Programmatic reaches the right person, right message, right time." Reframe: programmatic doesn't automatically create relevance; it gives infrastructure to make millions of real-time decisions, only as intelligent as the data, signals, creative strategy, measurement framework, and business rules behind them. The value is coordinating signals, creative variations, inventory, frequency, timing, and bidding across a dynamic journey.
- **Wins / learnings:** Invest Vancouver (World Cup audience wrap-up); Whitecaps (reaching Fan Fest attendees); SkiBig3 BigQuery "Kedet" intelligence integration & Dynamic Audience framework; Inn at Laurel Point (revenue increase off a US travel policy — "Travel Local, Stay Local" campaign).
- **Team to feature:** Everyone.
- **News sources:** Google & Meta documentation; IAB Tech Lab; digiday.com; Search Engine Land.
- **Voices / competitors:** Thrive (podcasts); Cosset (design).
- **Uncomfortable truth:** Paid media cannot compensate for a weak product, unclear value proposition, poor customer experience, or ineffective creative. Advertising can only amplify what already exists.
- **"We want to be on every channel":** Presence on every channel ≠ effective strategy. Each channel needs sufficient budget, relevant creative, reliable measurement, and a clear journey role. Spread too thin and you get coverage without frequency, data, or scale. Prioritize the channels where audience, objective, creative format, and measurement are strongest; establish performance; then expand with a clear testing plan.
- **Most-asked (brand keywords):** "Why are you capturing my brand keywords?" Brand campaigns protect visibility from competitors, control messaging/landing pages, support promotions, capture high-intent searches, and show how branded demand changes over time. (Anecdote: a competitor brand-keyword takeover that disrupted a rival's luxury-SUV launch — "advertising is a war battle.")
- **Event:** Search Ads Advanced 102.
- **$50K — stop first:** Hard without an audit; all clients differ. Generalized: stop ignoring your customer's behaviour; listen to current signals; design a dynamic plan aligned to the organic discovery-to-purchase path; maximize results.

### R3-C
- **Widely-accepted idea that's wrong:** That AI and automation have made paid media "hands-off." Automation made execution easier, but strategy matters more than ever. Winners have stronger first-party data, better measurement, better creative testing, clearer objectives. AI optimizes what you give it — it doesn't fix poor strategy or bad data.
- **Best practice / warning label:** "Always follow platform recommendations." Recommendations maximize platform performance, not business performance. Auto-applied suggestions, broad expansion, and aggressive automation can work, but only with clean data and rigorous testing. Best practice: test before you trust.
- **Most-asked client questions:** How is AI changing paid search? How do we show up in Search Generative AI? How to measure when attribution is less reliable? Are Performance Max and Advantage+ worth it? How to improve lead quality vs. generate more leads? How to think about first-party data in a privacy-first world? How do we know if our agency drives incremental growth?
- **Programmatic wallpaper:** DSPs say "reach the right audience at the right time." Reframe: "Programmatic isn't about buying impressions — it's about buying probability." Every impression should increase the likelihood of a business outcome; success is whether data, creative, targeting, and measurement create incremental value.
- **Content ideas:** SGE and paid search; why lead quality is declining despite higher spend; how BigQuery changes marketing decision-making; when AI helps vs. adds noise; building an outbound engine vs. buying leads; CRM issues that hurt paid media; evaluating agencies beyond ROAS; GA4 + server-side migration lessons; technical marketing as competitive advantage; what enterprise marketers should measure in board meetings.
- **Team to feature:** [technical/analytics lead] (technical marketing, analytics, AI, measurement, data infrastructure); [client-strategy lead] (client strategy, retention, account growth); Sales team (objections, market trends, buying behavior); CX team (implementation, optimization, success stories).
- **News sources:** Search Engine Land, Search Engine Journal, AdExchanger, Marketing Brew, Digiday, The Drum, Adweek, Think with Google, Meta Business News, Microsoft Advertising Blog; OpenAI, Google AI Blog, Anthropic, Perplexity, Search Central, SEOFOMO, SparkToro; Measure Slack, CXL, Analytics Mania, BigQuery release notes, HubSpot.
- **Voices:** Avinash Kaushik, Rand Fishkin, Tom Critchlow, Simo Ahava; Kieran Flanagan, Dave Gerhardt, Peep Laja, April Dunford; Ethan Mollick, Allie K. Miller, OpenAI, Anthropic; Google Search Liaison, HubSpot Research, Ahrefs, Semrush.
- **Strong competitor content:** They publish *evidence*, not just content — original research, benchmarks, experiments, opinions backed by data. Directive (SaaS growth, data-backed); NP Digital (research/frameworks); Ahrefs (teaches, doesn't sell); HubSpot (original research); SparkToro (contrarian + evidence); MeasureSchool / Analytics Mania (approachable technical); Google (first-party docs).
- **Uncomfortable truth:** Most paid media problems aren't media problems. Weak creative, poor conversion experiences, unclear positioning, slow websites, broken CRM processes, or unrealistic expectations usually matter more than campaign optimization. Paid media amplifies the strengths and weaknesses of the entire customer journey.
- **"We want to be on every channel":** "I'd rather dominate two channels than underperform on eight." Every channel has a learning curve, creative requirements, measurement complexity, and budget needs. Spread too thin and nothing gets enough data to optimize. Start where the audience is, prove what works, expand strategically.
- **Most-asked question:** "How do we know if our marketing is actually working?" — not ROAS or lead count, but whether marketing drives incremental business growth. They don't know because platforms report what they can measure, not what matters; attribution is fragmented; privacy changed the rules; many orgs don't connect performance to revenue.
- **Event:** See internal 2026 sales conference / event doc.
- **$50K — stop first:** Stop optimizing to platform metrics. CTR, CPC, impressions, and ROAS tell part of the story. Look first at pipeline, revenue, CAC, incrementality, and LTV. Too many celebrate campaign metrics while missing whether marketing grows the business.
