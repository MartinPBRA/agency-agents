---

## Design direction (keep it one system)
So the whole set reads as one brand, not twelve one-offs:
- **Format:** 1080×1080 (feed) + 1080×1920 (story/reel cover) export of each.
- **Type:** one bold display weight for the quote, one light weight for the byline. Left-aligned.
- **Emphasis:** the "flip" half of every antithesis is the accent color — the *"not X"* stays neutral, the *"— Y"* pops. This visually teaches the signature move.
- **Byline:** "— War Room" bottom-left, small, consistent placement.
- **Restraint:** no stock photos. Type + one accent + generous negative space. The take is the hero.

### New Carousel A — "Paid media can't fix a bad product"
1. Paid media can't fix a bad product. Here's the uncomfortable truth. →
2. Advertising doesn't create value. It amplifies whatever is already there.
3. Strong product + offer + experience? Paid pours fuel on it.
4. Weak fundamentals? Paid still works — it just tells more people, faster.
5. "Our ads aren't working" is often the wrong diagnosis. The ads worked; the audience voted no.
6. Great marketing scales success. It doesn't create it. Follow War Room. — War Room

### New Carousel B — "The 3 DOOH metrics that actually matter"
1. Most DOOH reports measure the wrong thing. Here are the 3 that matter. →
2. #1 — Verified exposure, not booked impressions. Did it actually run, seen by real people?
3. #2 — Incremental lift, not attributed clicks. What changed *because* of the screen?
4. #3 — Cost per outcome, not cost per thousand. Tie the spend to a business result.
5. Impressions are the easiest number to report and the least useful to act on.
6. Measure outcomes, not opportunities-to-see. Follow War Room. — War Room
