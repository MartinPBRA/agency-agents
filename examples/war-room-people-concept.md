# War Room — "Human in the Loop" (People-First AI Concept)

> **What this is:** A recurring content concept that puts the **humans** front and centre and casts AI
> as the co-pilot, never the driver. It turns the brand's sharpest AI POVs into a visible, repeatable
> format — the belief *"AI helps us, it doesn't do the job"* made real through the people doing the work.

---

## The big idea
Everyone is automating, and we're not pretending otherwise — we use AI every day. But a tool doesn't understand your business, your customer, or your risk. People do. So the concept makes the human judgment layer the star.

**Concept name:** **Human in the Loop** — the AI term, repurposed to mean *there's always a person making the call.*
**Signature line:** *AI helps us. It doesn't do the job.*
**Recurring motif:** *hands on the wheel* (use as a closer/tagline within pieces).

**Mini-manifesto (for a pinned post / about section):**
> We use AI every day. It speeds up the reps — the analysis, the variations, the execution. But it doesn't understand your business, and it can't be accountable for your growth. So here's the deal: AI does the reps, our people make the calls. There's always a human in the loop.

---

## Why this works
- **Differentiation, not variation.** In a sea of "we use AI" sameness, showing the *people + judgment* is the ownable angle (it dramatizes flagship POV 23).
- **Trust & thought leadership.** Getting real team members out front is exactly the [Thrive](war-room-pov-library.md) insight the team admired — video walkthroughs that connect prospects to the humans they'd actually work with.
- **Proof of the belief.** It's the visible version of POV 1 (amplify, don't replace), POV 8 (strategy is the whole job), POV 2 / POV 10 (test before you trust the algorithm).

---

## The format menu (the series)
1. **"AI drafted. I decided."** — a team member shows what AI produced vs. the call they made, and why. *(carousel or short video)*
2. **Video walkthrough** — a strategist works through a real task using AI, narrating every judgment call. *(Thrive-style; highest-trust format)*
3. **"The override"** — a short story of a time we corrected the algorithm/AI and it paid off. *(ties to the TikTok "pause a winner" receipt)*
4. **A day in the loop** — where AI speeds things up, and where a human has to step in.
5. **Ask the operator** — a team member answers a real client question on camera.
6. **Meet-the-human spotlight** — a short profile: what they own, how they think, one strong opinion.

**Cadence:** one "Human in the Loop" piece every ~2 weeks (AI / Programmatic pillars) + a monthly team spotlight.

## Example executions

### A · "AI drafted. I decided." (LinkedIn post)
> AI wrote 40 versions of this campaign. We shipped none of them.
>
> To be clear — the 40 versions were good. Generated in minutes, clean, on-format. A year ago that's a full afternoon of work.
>
> But every one of them optimized for the wrong thing. They chased the metric the platform likes, not the outcome the client actually needs.
>
> So our strategist did the part the machine can't: looked at the client's real goal, and made a different call. The winning concept wasn't in the 40. It came from understanding the business behind the brief.
>
> That's the job now. AI does the reps. People make the calls.
>
> AI helps us. It doesn't do the job.
>
> — War Room

### B · "The override" (LinkedIn post)
> The algorithm told us to pause our best campaign. We didn't.
>
> Everything on the dashboard said stop — the automated recommendation was confident about it. But a person who actually understood the client's business looked closer and saw what the algorithm couldn't: that campaign was doing exactly what it was supposed to.
>
> We ignored the recommendation. It kept performing.
>
> Automation is a brilliant co-pilot. It's a terrible pilot. The moment you stop questioning it is the moment it quietly costs you.
>
> Hands on the wheel. Always.
>
> — War Room

### C · Video walkthrough (script outline)
- **Cold open (5s):** "Watch me build a [X] with AI in 10 minutes — and catch every place I overrule it."
- **Middle:** real task, real screen; each time AI suggests something, the human says *why* they accept or reject it.
- **Close:** "The AI did the typing. The judgment was mine. That's the whole point." → *AI helps us. It doesn't do the job.*

## One decision to make
This concept is built to **feature real team members** — that's the source of the trust. It's a deliberate shift from the anonymous "War Room" byline we've used elsewhere.
- **Recommended:** use real names + faces for this series (the human connection *is* the strategy), while keeping the anonymous byline on the opinion/POV posts.
- **Alternative:** keep it role-based ("our programmatic lead") if the team prefers privacy — weaker connection, but still on-concept.
- **Confirm:** who on the team opts in to being featured.
