## AI Overviews / AEO — the differentiator
Two of your respondents flagged AI Overviews as the #1 emerging client concern. War Room should practice what it preaches and get cited in AI answers first. What actually works:
- **Answer-first structure** — lead each page with a clear, direct answer to the question, then go deep. Extractable = citable.
- **Question-based H2s** that match how people (and AI) phrase queries.
- **Publish evidence, not opinions** — original data, benchmarks, and frameworks are what generative engines cite (this is the R3 content principle, applied to SEO).
- **Entity clarity + schema** — Article/FAQ/Organization markup so engines understand who's saying it.
- **Earn third-party citations** — get referenced by the sources AI pulls from (industry pubs, research roundups) via genuinely original data.

## The repurposing flywheel (social ⇄ search)
No content gets made once.
1. **Attention** — a sharp POV ships as a LinkedIn post (e.g. the AI flagship, "ROAS is the floor").
2. **Capture** — the same POV is expanded into a long-form article that targets the matching search query.
3. **Compound** — the article ranks and gets cited in AI Overviews; it keeps working long after the post scrolls away.
4. **Recirculate** — the article feeds the newsletter and gets re-cut back into social.

> One idea → one post → one ranking page → one newsletter section → months of compounding. That's the multiple SEO adds on top of social's spike.

## Measure it like you'd tell a client to
Consistent with War Room's own POV (*"stop optimizing to platform metrics"*): don't judge SEO on rankings alone.
- **Leading:** rankings, indexed pages, AI-Overview citations, organic impressions.
- **Lagging (what matters):** organic → pipeline, branded-search lift, inbound leads, assisted conversions.
- Tie organic to business outcomes the same way you reframed ROAS to revenue.
