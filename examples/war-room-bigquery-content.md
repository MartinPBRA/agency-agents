## The sharpened POV
Most agencies name-drop their data stack as if the tool is the value. It isn't. The antithesis:

> **"BigQuery didn't make us faster. Killing manual reporting did."**

BigQuery is the enabler; the win is the hours it handed back to strategists. That's the ownable take — every competitor can say "we use BigQuery"; almost none can say what they did with the time it freed.

**Supporting antitheses (house-voice reframes):**
- "BigQuery isn't a faster database — it's a warehouse that answers questions humans couldn't ask before."
- "BigQuery doesn't save you money — it saves you time, then bills you for the bytes."
- "The point was never the query. It was the reporting hour we stopped burning."

## Cluster 3 · Blog outline — "The honest guide to BigQuery for agencies"
- **Hook:** "We use BigQuery" is table stakes, not a moat. Here's the honest version.
- **What it's genuinely great at:** serverless scale, the gravity well of the Google stack (GA4, Ads Data Transfer), SQL-first so analysts move without a data-eng team behind them.
- **Where it bites:** per-byte-scanned billing (the footgun), warehouse ≠ transactional database, vendor gravity as real lock-in.
- **The discipline that separates winners:** partitioning, clustering, materialized rollups, killing auto-refresh dashboards — framed as cost strategy, not IT chores.
- **The real payoff (proof):** the SkiBig3 automation — reporting time reclaimed, strategists back on strategy.
- **Close:** the antithesis — it didn't make us faster; killing manual reporting did.
